# Annual operating energy savings of liquid desiccant and evaporative-cooling-assisted 100% outdoor air system

- **Authors**: Jae-Weon Jeong, Hye-Jin Cho, Su-Gwang Jeong, Soolyeon Cho
- **Journal**: Energy and Buildings, Vol. 76, pp. 569–583
- **Publication Year**: 2014
- **DOI**: [10.1016/j.enbuild.2014.03.006](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.enbuild.2014.03.006)
- **Keywords / Tags**: #LD_DOAS #EnergySavings #100OutdoorAir #DecoupledCooling

---

### 1. Executive Summary (BLUF)
- Evaluates the annual energy consumption of a 100% dedicated outdoor air system (DOAS) assisted by a liquid desiccant unit and indirect/direct evaporative cooling (IDEC) versus a conventional variable air volume (VAV) cooling coil dehumidification system with reheat in Seoul, Korea.
- Confirms **51% annual operating energy reduction** by eliminating deep chilled-water subcooling (to condense moisture) and subsequent reheat.

### 2. Key Findings & Quantitative Data
- **Reheat Elimination**: Conventional DOAS chills outdoor air down to 10-12 °C (dew point) and reheats it to 18-20 °C. The LD-DOAS separates latent load (absorbed isothermally by the desiccant) from sensible load, requiring zero reheat energy.
- **Cooling Plant COP Improvement**: In LD-DOAS, the sensible cooling coils can use high-temperature chilled water (14-17 °C) instead of low-temperature water (5-7 °C), boosting chiller COP significantly.

### 3. Engineering Takeaways for CrecoPLUS DOAS
- Establishes the business case and energy savings rationale for replacing chilled-water condensation dehumidifiers with liquid desiccant DOAS in humid temperate climates (such as Korea).
