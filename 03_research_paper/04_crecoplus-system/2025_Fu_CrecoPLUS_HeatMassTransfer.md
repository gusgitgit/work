# Heat and mass transfer in an internally cooled ionic liquid dehumidification system: Experimental study and empirical modeling

- **Authors**: Ben-Ran Fu, Wen-Wei Hsueh, Chih-Hao Chen, Shuo Wang
- **Journal**: Case Studies in Thermal Engineering, Vol. 76, 107483
- **Publication Year**: 2025
- **DOI**: [10.1016/j.csite.2025.107483](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.csite.2025.107483)
- **Keywords / Tags**: #CrecoPLUS5100c #InternallyCooled #SherwoodNumber #NusseltNumber #WettingEffect

---

### 1. Executive Summary (BLUF)
- Directly investigates an internally-cooled dehumidifier using **Crecoplus 5100c** ionic liquid as working fluid in a vertical counter-flow fin-tube heat exchanger.
- Proves that surface wetting on fin-tube coils is the dominant factor determining dehumidification effectiveness: increasing IL flow rate enhanced wetting area, **increasing dehumidification efficiency by up to 238% and mass transfer coefficient by over threefold**.
- Develops empirical dimensionless correlations for Sherwood ($\\mathrm{Sh}$) and Nusselt ($\\mathrm{Nu}$) numbers.

### 2. Key Experimental Findings
- **Cooling Water Effect**: Lower cooling water temperatures and higher flow rates maintain low solution surface vapor pressure, steepening the mass transfer gradient.
- **Solution Flow Rate & Wetting**: At low IL flow rates, high viscosity causes channeling and dry spots on the fin surface. High IL flow rates achieve >90% dehumidification efficiency.
- **Correlations**: Sherwood number correlation achieved MAD of 29.5%; Nusselt correlation achieved MAD of 1.4%.

### 3. Engineering Takeaways for CrecoPLUS DOAS
- **Contactor Architecture**: Fin-tube internally cooled configuration is superior to adiabatic packed towers because it removes absorption heat in situ.
- **Control Strategy**: Maintain IL circulation flow rate high and constant to preserve full fin wetting; control dehumidification capacity by modulating internal cooling water temperature.
